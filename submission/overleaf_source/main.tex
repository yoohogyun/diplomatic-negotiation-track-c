\begin{abstract}
Can explicit strategic control improve negotiation with a lower-cost language model? We study a simulation grounded in 12 real diplomatic documents, holding GPT-5.6 Luna's generated utterances and their interpretations fixed across controllers. On 64 matched conditions, belief-state planning achieves net utility 0.533, compared with 0.514 for direct action selection by GPT-5.6 Sol with medium reasoning. Planner-distilled PPO reaches 0.475 without further model calls for strategy selection. Separate interventions attribute most of the learned policy's gain to signals and updated beliefs. Dialogue analysis connects these mechanisms to conditional offers and commitment wording. The results identify the benefits of explicit control under declared negotiation rules.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language model (LLM) agents create opportunities to negotiate on behalf of users \citep{yao2023react,bianchi2024negotiationarena}. They must pursue user goals while responding to parties with different interests and incomplete information. Existing benchmarks show that strategic choices affect outcomes \citep{abdelnabi2024stakeholders}. Repeated decisions also make inference cost relevant \citep{kapoor2024agents}, motivating systems that assign specialized roles to lower-cost models \citep{belcak2025small}.

Prior work separates strategy from language generation \citep{he-etal-2018-decoupling}, combines dialogue with planning \citep{fair2022cicero}, and improves smaller negotiators through reinforcement learning and distillation \citep{hua2026socialrl,tabacof2026negotiate}. Building on these approaches, we ask: with language realization held fixed, how does explicit strategic control affect negotiation utility and the cost of choosing actions?

We construct a diplomatic negotiation simulation from 12 real documents across six event groups. The documents provide context and examples of reciprocal conditions; utilities, hidden opponent types, and responses are synthetic. GPT-5.6 Luna (Luna) generates and separately interprets 396 fixed utterances. We compare Random, Heuristic, Bandit, PPO, direct model decisions, an exact public-belief planner, and a policy distilled from that planner (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). The planner knows the simulation rules but never observes the hidden type.

On 64 matched conditions, the planner achieves higher utility than direct action selection by GPT-5.6 Sol (Sol) with medium reasoning. Distilled PPO improves on base PPO and acts without further model calls for strategy selection. We then use interventions and matched dialogue traces to explain these results. This analysis distinguishes questions that reveal information from signals that change acceptance incentives, and examines whether actions preserve the conditions and commitments expressed in dialogue. Our contribution is a controlled comparison linking negotiation outcomes, strategic computation, and discourse meaning.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Negotiation agents.}
\citet{lewis-etal-2017-deal} learn from human bargaining dialogues and improve decisions through simulated continuations. Recent benchmarks cover resource allocation, trading, and multiple stakeholders \citep{bianchi2024negotiationarena,abdelnabi2024stakeholders}. CICERO combines dialogue and strategic reasoning in the board game Diplomacy \citep{fair2022cicero}. Our simulation uses real diplomatic documents to frame reciprocal measures and commitments.

\paragraph{Strategic control.}
\citet{he-etal-2018-decoupling} separate dialogue acts from linguistic realization. GDP-Zero uses LLM simulations for tree search \citep{yu-etal-2023-prompt}; PPDPP learns a separate policy planner \citep{deng2024ppdpp}; and Dual-Mind combines strategy planning with expression refinement \citep{liu-etal-2025-dual}. We build on this separation by holding utterances and interpretations fixed when comparing controllers, then analyzing their information use and commitments.

\paragraph{Efficient agents.}
\citet{kapoor2024agents} advocate evaluating performance alongside cost, while \citet{belcak2025small} argue for specialized small-model roles. SocialRL combines reinforcement learning and teacher distillation for negotiation \citep{hua2026socialrl}. \citet{tabacof2026negotiate} study reinforcement learning across seller model sizes. We keep language model weights fixed and train a separate controller, measuring strategy-selection calls and examining the mechanisms behind utility gains.
