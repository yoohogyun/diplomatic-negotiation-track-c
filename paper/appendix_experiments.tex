\section{Environment and Data}
\label{app:environment}
\paragraph{Payoffs and response model.}
The agent's terminal agreement utility and counterpart's acceptance utility are
\begin{align}
 u_A(x,y)&=0.5y-0.18x,\\
 u_B(x,y,\tau,z)&=0.35x-c_\tau y+\delta z.
\end{align}
Here $c_S=0.2$, $c_H$ is configuration-specific, and $z$ indicates whether a signal has been sent. The counterpart accepts if $u_B\geq0$. Ratification additionally requires observed acceptance and $u_A\geq0.12$. Withdrawal or expiration returns terminal utility 0.12 before accumulated costs. Agreement rates throughout the paper refer to this implemented terminal transition.

For the first private question, $P(o=H\mid H)=q$ and $P(o=H\mid S)=1-q$. Later questions cost the same but provide no new cue. Offers receive deterministic acceptance or rejection conditional on type. For likelihoods $L_H$ and $L_S$ of the observed response,
\begin{equation}
 b_{t+1}=\frac{b_t L_H}{b_t L_H+(1-b_t)L_S}.
\end{equation}
The hidden type generates responses but is never an input to any controller. Beliefs are updated by the environment from interpreted meanings and responses. Equal type likelihoods leave the belief unchanged.

\paragraph{Costs and commitments.}
Each decision costs 0.01. Questions incur $c_q$; signals incur the expenditure extracted from their utterance (intended cost 0.06). Sending a signal sets $z=1$; repeated signals incur cost but do not stack their benefit. Each rejected offer incurs $c_r$ and increments escalation $e$. Every turn also costs $0.005e_{t+1}^2$. Reducing one's offered duration below the recorded commitment costs 0.08. Ambiguity makes the next offer provisional, preventing that offer from replacing the recorded public commitment; it does not erase an earlier commitment or remove a reversal charge. Invalid interpretations and invalid ratification each cost 0.10. Rewards sum terminal utility, when present, minus these costs. We report undiscounted net utility even when training uses discounted returns.

\paragraph{Public features.}
The 31-dimensional observation includes time, posterior belief, offer durations, acceptance, signal and question flags, provisional status, commitment, escalation, prior, question reliability, numerical utilities and costs, a three-way style indicator, and a seven-way indicator of the last interpreted function. It excludes source identity and hidden type. Direct-model prompts receive the public state, configuration, last interpreted function, and the numerical decision rules. Each unique prompted state has one saved model response; this is not repeated sampling over model outputs.

\paragraph{Language checks.}
The three styles are a formal communiqu\'e, a direct private statement, and a concise statement with reordered clauses. Intended and interpreted meanings match in 379 of 396 entries; all 17 mismatches remain in the environment. In 180 controlled probes covering valid offers, changed durations, negation, unless-conditions, and missing reciprocity, the interpreter matches the authored expectation in all cases. These are automatic consistency checks. They do not establish human-validated speech-act accuracy. Twelve source contexts also have two type-specific validation paths each (24 total).

The heuristic is selected from six fixed candidates using development net utility. The selected policy sends a signal before choosing an offer with high belief-weighted immediate value, ratifies eligible agreements, and withdraws when its rule favors the outside option. Random samples uniformly from 11 actions. The contextual Bandit uses a linear immediate-reward estimator per action with exploration probability 0.2.

Base PPO uses two 64-unit hidden layers, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, discount 0.95, entropy coefficient 0.01, rollout length 256, batch size 64, and ten optimization epochs. Bandit and PPO use training seeds 17, 29, and 43. Random uses evaluation seeds 101, 202, and 303 with four repetitions per response branch and style.

The planner enumerates transitions using the actual interpreted utterance set for each context and style. It solves the finite-horizon problem by backward recursion, averaging response branches under the current public belief. The no-question planner excludes actions interpreted as questions. This planner has exact knowledge of the declared simulation and utterance meanings.

Distillation uses 43,440 public-state examples from training contexts and numerical settings, collected from planner trajectories and trajectories with 35\% random exploration. Targets are the planner's eleven action values $Q(a)$ and maximum value. The actor target is $p(a)\propto\exp(Q(a)/0.03)$. Training minimizes cross-entropy plus 0.5 times value mean-squared error, using 2,400 updates of 256 samples, Adam learning rate 0.001, and gradient clipping at 0.5. The same network then receives 102,400 PPO interactions. Development utility averaged over three seeds selects distilled PPO (0.465) over distillation alone (0.427). Teacher computation is additional to the interaction budget.

At evaluation, learned controllers choose their highest-scoring action. Blocking a question or signal excludes its action index and selects the highest remaining logit. Belief masking replaces only the posterior feature by the original prior. Other public observations, including accepted offers, remain visible. These interventions do not remove every possible source of type information and do not retrain the controller.

\paragraph{Aggregation and uncertainty.}
For each document--configuration condition, we enumerate both latent types and all nonzero first-question response branches with their declared probabilities. We average styles, then configurations and contexts within event groups, then event groups equally. The reported SD quantifies variation across three training seeds, or evaluation seeds for Random. It is not a confidence interval over historical events. Initial and follow-up evaluations each contain 256 document--configuration conditions, not 256 independent diplomatic events. Each matched method has 200 nonzero latent/response paths across its 64 conditions.

\paragraph{Supplementary training interventions.}
The initial experiment varies the discount (0.8, 0.95, 0.99), entropy coefficient (0, 0.01, 0.1), and inclusion of rejection and commitment-reversal costs during training. All variants are evaluated under the common original reward. Non-base variants use seed 17 and are exploratory. Their net utilities are 0.346 and 0.327 for discounts 0.8 and 0.99; 0.366 and 0.392 for entropy 0 and 0.1; 0.377 without training rejection costs; and 0.371 without training reversal costs. The corresponding seed-17 baseline and complete metrics are preserved in \texttt{study\_results/seed\_metrics.csv}. Reward components, counterpart utility, agreement, escalation, reversals, length, learning curves, and action-transition counts are included in the released results. The initial evaluation's weighted feasible fraction is 95.3\%; the follow-up fraction is 97.3\% under canonical offer and signal options. No reported terminal agreement violates the numerical bilateral acceptance checks.

\section{Discourse Cases}
\label{app:discourse}
We examine six stored episodes: four policies under one Iran-context condition, and a pair of Syria-context episodes with the same policy and numerical condition but different utterance styles. These are synthetic, source-grounded exchanges selected to explain decision paths and interpretation effects. They are not historical transcripts or a blinded human annotation study. The hidden types below describe evaluation branches, not information available to the agent.

\paragraph{Iran source and shared condition.}
Context \texttt{ir1} uses the 30 June 2020 Security Council passage S/2020/644 attributed in the supplied corpus to Halimah DeShong. The passage describes Iran's steps as reversible following US withdrawal from the agreement. Our authored setting uses conditional sanctions suspension and nuclear-activity suspension. In condition 0, the actual evaluation type is soft, $b_0=0.65$, $q=1$, $c_q=0.02$, $c_r=0.16$, $\delta=0.10$, and $c_H=0.45$; style 0 is fixed across policies.

\paragraph{Episode 1: Luna none.}
The agent says, ``We will suspend the specified sanctions for 1 week, conditional upon your suspension of the specified nuclear activities for 2 weeks; our commitment is contingent upon yours.'' The offer is rejected. The same text is repeated three more times, followed by withdrawal. Both types reject the unsignaled offer, so every rejection leaves $b=0.65$. Repetition therefore adds no type information while accumulating rejection and escalation costs. Terminal outside utility 0.12 minus total costs 0.92 yields net utility $-0.80$.

\paragraph{Episode 2: Belief-state planner.}
The planner asks, ``Privately, we ask whether you require a larger reciprocal concession before accepting this understanding. This inquiry is exploratory only and constitutes neither an offer nor a public commitment.'' A soft response changes $b$ to zero. The agent then commits resources at stated cost 0.06 to a monitored confidence-building measure. The same 1:2 offer is now accepted: the signal changes soft-type utility from $0.35-0.2(2)=-0.05$ to 0.05. The question identifies the type, while the signal changes the acceptance incentive. Agreement utility 0.82 minus four turn costs, question cost, and signal cost yields 0.70.

\paragraph{Episode 3: Sol medium.}
Sol selects the same question--signal--offer--ratification path and receives 0.70. Since all methods use the shared utterances, identical actions produce identical text here. This trace shows agreement between two controllers under the same state and language conditions; it is not a second independently generated dialogue style.

\paragraph{Episode 4: Distilled PPO, seed 17.}
The learned policy sends the signal without asking a question, then offers to suspend sanctions for two weeks if the counterpart suspends nuclear activities for the same period. Acceptance identifies the soft type in this condition, reducing $b$ from 0.65 to zero. The policy then selects ratification. Its larger concession reduces terminal utility to 0.64. Three turn costs and signal expenditure leave net utility 0.55. This path obtains information through an offer, but pays for it through less favorable terms.

\paragraph{Commitment strength in Episodes 2--4.}
The final shared utterance begins ``We reaffirm our readiness to ratify the currently accepted agreement.'' The interpreter assigns \texttt{ratify}, so the simulator records agreement. Expressing readiness, however, does not itself perform ratification. The continuation also imports source-context wording about reversibility and withdrawal that is absent from the preceding synthetic exchange. These features distinguish the implemented terminal decision from the utterance's commitment strength. We retain this mismatch in the analysis and do not silently correct the reported outcomes.

\paragraph{Syria source and shared condition.}
Context \texttt{sy1} draws on a 1 March 2016 briefing by Hong Lei urging implementation of the ceasefire and cessation of violence under Security Council Resolution 2254. In condition 25, the actual evaluation type is hard, $b_0=0.35$, $q=0.75$, $c_r=0.16$, $\delta=0$, and $c_H=0.9$. Both episodes use the signal-first heuristic. The hard type has no offer that simultaneously meets the agent's outside-option threshold and its own acceptance condition.

\paragraph{Episode 5: Explicit withdrawal, style 0.}
The agent sends a signal even though its configured effect is zero, then makes a 2:2 offer. Rejection changes $b$ from 0.35 to one. It then says, ``We hereby withdraw from these negotiations, without proposing or ratifying any agreement,'' followed by an appeal to uphold the ceasefire. The interpreter assigns \texttt{withdraw}, ending the episode with net utility $-0.14$. Withdrawal responds to learned infeasibility, but the earlier signal incurs an avoidable cost.

\paragraph{Episode 6: Conditional withdrawal, style 2.}
After the same signal--offer--rejection sequence, the selected withdrawal text is ``If these negotiations cannot advance without compromising our principles, we will withdraw without proposing or ratifying any agreement.'' The conditional threat does not execute an immediate withdrawal under the interpreter. It is classified as invalid and repeated for the remaining three decisions. The episode expires with net utility $-0.47$, a loss of 0.33 relative to Episode 5. This difference comes from three invalid-action charges plus two extra turn and escalation charges. The discourse distinction between leaving and conditionally threatening to leave therefore changes the realized trajectory.
