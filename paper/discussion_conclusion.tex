\section{Discussion}
Explicit control improves decisions when negotiation rules can be specified. The planner accounts for beliefs, costs, and future responses. Distillation transfers part of this behavior to a policy without online search or model calls. Its remaining gap to Sol medium and small advantage over the heuristic distinguish exact planning from learned control.

Signals drive more of the learned gain than questions. Offer responses can also update beliefs, so information value depends on subsequent choices and acquisition costs. Discourse analysis adds a separate requirement: utterances must preserve the selected conditions and commitment strength. Evaluation should connect these meanings to utility and information use.

\section{Conclusion}
With Luna utterances held fixed, belief-state planning achieved the highest matched utility, and planner-distilled PPO improved on base PPO without further model calls for action selection. Signals, updated beliefs, and commitment wording explain different parts of the observed behavior. This controlled study identifies explicit strategic control as a useful component for source-grounded diplomatic negotiation simulations.
